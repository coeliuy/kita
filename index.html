How to use Kita

Ten minutes to set up, then a few seconds a day.

  1. Add your clients

    Choose how you bill each one: hourly, a monthly retainer with included hours, or a fixed project fee.

  2. Set up categories and tasks

    Create categories for the kinds of work you do, then add tasks for each client with an optional estimate, due date or weekly repeat.

  3. Track your time

    Press play on a task, or start a timer from scratch. When you stop, add a note and mark the task done if it’s finished.

  4. Check in each week

    Overview shows your hours, capacity, retainer usage and insights on your rates and estimates.

  5. Invoice and report at month end

    Kita fills in invoices from your tracked time, grouped by category. Download the PDF, and send retainer clients their monthly report.

  6. Keep an eye on your money

    Log expenses as they happen, add any salary, set a goal for the year and move the suggested amount into your tax savings.

Questions

Where is my data kept?

Your clients, tasks, time and invoices are private to your account. You can download everything as a backup from Settings at any time.

Can I import meetings from my calendar?

Yes. Export your calendar as an .ics file from Google Calendar, Outlook or Apple Calendar, and Kita turns recent meetings into time entries, matching them to clients where it can. The file is read on your device and isn’t uploaded.

How accurate is the tax estimate?

It’s an estimate, not advice. It uses the 2026/27 rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, includes Class 4 National Insurance, and accounts for a salary if you add one. Like HMRC’s default cash basis, it counts income when you’re paid. It doesn’t yet cover Scottish tax bands, student loans or pension relief.

Does it work with Making Tax Digital?

Kita groups your income and expenses into the Making Tax Digital quarters and HMRC’s expense categories, and exports them as a CSV. It doesn’t send updates to HMRC directly.

Can I use my data in Google Sheets?

Yes. Download your time entries or quarterly totals as a CSV, then import the file into Sheets.
